Where Apple devices usually go wrong

In many companies Macs are the exception. They are set up by hand, everyone has admin rights, and updates wait until someone remembers.

That works for five Macs. At fifty it becomes a security gap and a time sink, and it is one of the first things a customer security questionnaire asks about.

What's included

  • Apple Business ManagerWe set up or tidy your account, link your resellers and assign devices to the right management server.
  • Zero-touch enrollmentNew devices enroll the first time they start, before the user reaches the desktop.
  • Sign-in with work accountsMac sign-in connected to Microsoft Entra ID or Google Workspace, so passwords stay in sync.
  • Encryption and recovery keysFileVault turned on for every Mac, with recovery keys stored in your management console.
  • Updates on your schedulemacOS and iOS updates enforced with deadlines you choose, not left to each user.
  • Self Service appsAn app catalogue where staff install approved software without admin rights.
  • Wi-Fi, VPN and certificatesNetwork settings pushed to devices, so nobody types a Wi-Fi password again.
  • Security baselineSettings based on CIS Benchmarks and the macOS Security Compliance Project, tuned to how you work.
  • Lost and stolen devicesLock a device, find an iPhone or iPad with Lost Mode, or erase it remotely.

How it runs

  1. Audit

Questions

Do we have to buy devices from a particular seller?

Devices bought from Apple or an authorised reseller that takes part in Apple Business Manager are added to your account automatically. Devices bought elsewhere can usually be added with Apple Configurator.

Can existing Macs be enrolled without erasing them?

Usually yes. iPhones and iPads normally need a reset to become fully supervised company devices, so we schedule those around your team.

Jamf Pro or Addigy: which should we use?

Jamf Pro gives the deepest control and suits larger or more complex fleets. Addigy has a simpler console that suits smaller teams. We recommend one after the audit, based on your size, budget and skills.

Who owns the licences?

You do. Licences can be bought directly from the vendor or through a reseller, and the account always stays in your company's name.
